# Insulin Resistance & Mitochondrial Function

## The system

Insulin resistance is not only “too much sugar.” Muscle and liver cells that cannot make or use ATP cleanly also handle glucose poorly. Insulin is the key that is supposed to move GLUT4 glucose transporters to the cell surface; when the energy machinery behind that door is damaged — oxidative stress, stiff mitochondrial membranes, low AMPK tone — glucose stays in the blood even if insulin is present. That is why this map is split into three jobs: restore a glucose-uptake program that does not depend only on insulin, protect the mitochondrial membrane that program runs on, and take pressure off the system by lowering the metabolic load that created the problem.

Diet, muscle work, sleep, and any prescribed glucose medicine still do more of this job than any peptide list. This page maps compounds. It does not replace those.

## Category one — a glucose-uptake program that is not only insulin

**MOTS-c** is the center of this map for a specific reason. In the research literature it activates AMPK through an AICAR-type signal, which can move GLUT4 to the membrane without waiting on the insulin receptor. That is insulin-*independent* glucose uptake in muscle — demonstrated clearly in animals, not yet dose-mapped in a healthy-adult trial of injectable MOTS-c. Same cascade also leans the cell toward fat oxidation and mitochondrial biogenesis (PGC-1α). If this page only kept one peptide, it would be this one.

## Category two — the membrane the program runs on

**SS-31** does not copy MOTS-c. It binds cardiolipin on the inner mitochondrial membrane and is studied as protection for the electron-transport chain that actually makes ATP. MOTS-c is closer to “rewrite the energy program.” SS-31 is closer to “keep the wiring from shorting.” Pairing them is mechanistic logic from the KVA operating picture, not a combined human insulin-resistance trial.

**NAD+** is the charge carrier those pathways use (sirtuins, redox). Biochemistry is real. Injectable NAD+ as a proven insulin-sensitizer in humans is not. It sits here as support for the first two jobs, not as a third copy of MOTS-c.

## Category three — lowering the load that keeps the signal broken

**Retatrutide** is the odd one in, on purpose. It does not rewire AMPK the way MOTS-c does. It changes intake, nutrient partitioning, and body-fat mass through GLP-1 / GIP / glucagon signaling — and unlike MOTS-c it has sizable human metabolic data. Visceral fat is an inflammatory and insulin-resistant organ, not inert storage. This category is “take pressure off the system,” at a different confidence level than category one.

## Markers people track

These are readouts, not a start-of-protocol checklist:

- Fasting insulin and fasting glucose together (one without the other hides the story)
- HOMA-IR as a derived index, not a diagnosis
- Triglycerides and HDL
- HbA1c when a clinician is already following it
- GGT as a rough metabolic / liver stress companion

A single fasting glucose in the “normal” range does not rule this picture out.

## Oral supports that actually map

Named because the biochemistry is ordinary and human-use data is thicker than for the peptides — still not a prescription:

- Magnesium (glycinate or bisglycinate) — cofactor in insulin signaling
- Berberine — AMPK-adjacent glucose handling
- Alpha-lipoic acid — redox / glucose-disposal conversation
- Zinc — if it is actually low
- Chromium — thinner evidence; optional, not required

Vanadyl sulfate appeared in the source recipes for a short window. The *job* is real: vanadium can act as an insulin-mimetic by inhibiting PTP1B, the phosphatase that turns the insulin receptor back off. Older human work with vanadyl sulfate was mixed (GI side effects; one tolerated-dose study did not clearly improve sensitivity). Related organic vanadium compounds reached trials and ran into kidney-safety problems. So the mechanism is curious and documented — and it still does not earn a slot on the public list next to berberine and magnesium. Left as a footnote, not a recommendation.

## Evidence tier

**Retatrutide** — human metabolic outcome data exists on its own card; that is not the same as a trial of “Reta + MOTS-c + SS-31.”  
**MOTS-c** — Tier 2 for this page: real AMPK / GLUT4 / PGC-1α pathway work, almost entirely animal and mechanistic for the injectable used in practice.  
**SS-31** — Tier 2 for membrane protection; insulin-resistance outcomes in general-population humans are not the Barth-syndrome / trial story on its card.  
**NAD+** — established cofactor biochemistry; weak as a standalone insulin-resistance intervention.

No combined human trial of this pairing exists. Each compound card carries the sourcing.

## What this does not cover

Type 1 diabetes, hypoglycemia from prescribed insulin or secretagogues, medication changes, or “peptides instead of food, muscle, and sleep.” A rising A1c or symptoms of hyperosmolar crisis belong in clinic care, not on this page.

## Related compounds

MOTS-c, SS-31, NAD+, Retatrutide. Doses and cycling live on those cards.
