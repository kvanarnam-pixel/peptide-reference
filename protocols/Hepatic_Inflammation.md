# Hepatic Inflammation & Liver Support

## The system

The liver is both victim and amplifier of metabolic inflammation. Enzymes rise when fat, alcohol, drugs, viral injury, or bile-flow problems load hepatocytes faster than they can clear oxidative work. This page maps compounds to that load — metabolic pressure, mitochondrial redox, inflammatory tone, and a gut–liver repair backdrop.

It is not a hepatitis protocol, not a fibrosis-outcome claim, and not a substitute for finding *why* the enzymes moved.

## Category one — take pressure off the organ (primary when fat mass is the driver)

**Retatrutide** is not presented here as a liver therapy. It changes intake, partitioning, and visceral fat through GLP-1 / GIP / glucagon signaling, and it has human metabolic data the other names on this page do not. Visceral fat and hepatic fat travel together. This category is load reduction, at a higher outcome-confidence level than the mitochondrial pair — and still not a labeled NASH indication in this app.

## Category two — the energy chemistry the hepatocyte runs on (primary)

**MOTS-c** maps here for AMPK / fuel-flexibility, the same job as the insulin-resistance page. A hepatocyte stuck in poor fuel handling is part of the enzyme story more often than people want to admit.

**SS-31** protects the inner-membrane architecture (cardiolipin) that ATP production actually uses. High-energy tissue, same wiring-crew logic. Not a liver-outcome trial.

**NAD+** is the redox / sirtuin carrier those pathways use. Support for category two, not a third MOTS-c.

## Category three — quieting and the gut–liver backdrop (support, or primary when the lining is the source)

**KPV** interrupts NF-κB-type inflammatory signaling. Useful when the enzyme bump sits on a loud inflammatory picture — not when the only finding is a slightly high ALT after a week of training.

**BPC-157** is not a liver drug. The job it *can* do is the gut–liver axis: a beaten-up barrier dumps work onto the portal circulation. Repair coordinator on that side of the wall. Preclinical organ-protection language exists and is still not a human hepatitis trial.

## Named as support — card not built for the injectable

**Glutathione** is the liver’s own redox buffer. The biochemistry is ordinary and the oral precursors (NAC, glycine) already sit on the support list. Injectable glutathione showed up in the source recipes as a direct-GSH move. There is no glutathione card in this app. Keep it as a support job: replenish the buffer the hepatocyte is spending. Do not read this as a dosing page or as a fatty-liver outcome claim for IV glutathione.

## Markers people track

- ALT, AST, and GGT together (GGT is the one people skip)
- Platelets and a fibrosis index such as FIB-4 when the question is “is this stiffening,” not when one ALT is 42
- Fasting insulin, triglycerides — metabolic spillover
- Ferritin as an acute-phase overlay, not an iron protocol by itself

Enzymes that fall after a week off alcohol or after weight loss are information. Enzymes that stay up need a clinician, not a longer peptide list.

## Oral supports that actually map

Named because the source papers leaned as hard on this list as on the injectables — still not a prescription:

- NAC and glycine — glutathione precursors
- TUDCA — bile-flow conversation
- Milk thistle (silymarin)
- Choline
- Taurine
- Alpha-lipoic acid
- Omega-3
- Magnesium glycinate
- Methylfolate / methylcobalamin if a clinician has already framed a methylation gap
- Vitamin E — thinner, optional, not a default add

## Evidence tier

**Retatrutide** — human metabolic data; liver-fat change in that research is a downstream reading, not a standalone liver-indication card.  
**MOTS-c / SS-31 / NAD+** — Tier 2 pathway logic.  
**KPV / BPC-157** — support / barrier jobs.  
**Glutathione** — established biochemistry; injectable as a liver-outcome tool is not mapped here.

No combined human trial of this pairing.

## What this does not cover

Acute hepatitis, biliary obstruction, alcohol withdrawal, acetaminophen injury, or “enzymes came down so the liver is fine.” Also not a reason to stop a prescribed statin or antiviral because this list exists.

## Related compounds

Retatrutide, MOTS-c, SS-31, NAD+, KPV, BPC-157. Glutathione is named as support; no card. Doses live on the cards that exist.
