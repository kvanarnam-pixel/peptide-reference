# Systemic Inflammation (Elevated hsCRP)

## The system

hsCRP — high-sensitivity C-reactive protein — is a blood marker the liver produces in response to one signal in particular: interleukin-6 (IL-6), an inflammatory messenger released whenever the body detects ongoing tissue stress. hsCRP itself doesn't cause anything; it's a readout. A persistently elevated number usually means something upstream is keeping the IL-6 signal switched on longer than it should be — old injuries that never fully resolved, low-grade metabolic stress, oxidative burden, fat tissue acting as an inflammatory source, or an immune system that's stuck partly "on." Because there are several genuinely distinct ways that signal stays elevated, no single mechanism covers the whole picture — which is the actual reason this pairing runs six compounds deep, organized into three separate categories rather than one long undifferentiated list.

## Three categories, not one mechanism repeated six times

### Category one — suppressing the signal at its source

These three interrupt the NF-κB pathway itself, the transcription factor that switches on IL-6 production, each at a different point in the chain.

**KPV** blocks the last step before the inflammatory genes get switched on — it physically prevents the NF-κB signaling complex from being transported into the cell's nucleus.

**MOTS-c** reaches the same transcription factor from further upstream. Its AMPK-activation cascade suppresses NF-κB and the related NLRP3 inflammasome before the signal even reaches the point KPV interrupts. Two compounds hitting the same factor from different nodes isn't redundant — it's coverage of a pathway with more than one activation route.

**GHK-Cu** contributes on two related fronts: a documented reduction in TNF-α-induced IL-6 secretion directly, plus copper delivery to superoxide dismutase, the cell's own antioxidant enzyme — and oxidative stress is itself one of the things that activates NF-κB in the first place.

### Category two — resolving inflammation once it's already happened

These two don't prevent the inflammatory signal from firing — they shorten how long it stays active once it has.

**BPC-157** doesn't block TNF-α/IL-6/IL-1β signaling the way category-one compounds do — it lets the acute response happen, then actively shifts the signaling environment toward IL-10 and context-appropriate TGF-β, the body's own resolution program. That's a different job from suppression: turning the fire off once it's done its job, rather than never letting it start.

**TB-500** works on the same "resolution" side from a cellular-mobilization angle — moving repair cells to injury sites faster and shifting local macrophages from a damage-signaling state (M1) toward a repair-signaling state (M2). Tissue damage that resolves faster generates an inflammatory trigger for a shorter window.

### Category three — reducing an upstream inflammatory source

**Retatrutide** is the odd one out here, deliberately. It doesn't touch the NF-κB pathway or the resolution phase at all. Its likely contribution is upstream and indirect: fat tissue — particularly visceral and hepatic fat — is itself an active secretor of inflammatory cytokines, not just inert storage. Meaningful fat mass reduction lowers that source of cytokine production directly. This is included at a different confidence level than the other five: the supporting evidence is largely from an animal steatohepatitis model rather than a direct human hsCRP trial, and it's about removing a contributor to the problem rather than intercepting an inflammatory pathway. Worth including, worth being honest about what kind of evidence it rests on.

## Evidence tier

Stated plainly rather than implying more certainty than exists: **none of these six compounds has a completed human trial measuring hsCRP as its endpoint.** KPV's and MOTS-c's NF-κB interference are each Tier 2 — mechanistically plausible, sourced to real pathway studies, but pathway evidence rather than a human anti-inflammatory-outcome trial. GHK-Cu's TNF-α/IL-6 finding is a real, documented cell-culture result; its antioxidant contribution via SOD is established biochemistry. BPC-157's shift toward IL-10/TGF-β is documented mechanistic behavior, Tier 2. TB-500's contribution is Tier 2 leaning toward Tier 3 — sound physiological reasoning rather than something measured directly against hsCRP. Retatrutide's inflammatory-marker evidence is real but currently the least direct of the six — primarily animal-model, hepatic-tissue-specific, and mechanistically downstream of fat-mass reduction rather than a primary anti-inflammatory action. Each compound's own reference card carries the full detail and sourcing behind these summaries.

## What this protocol does not cover

Elevated hsCRP has upstream drivers this list does nothing to address — untreated metabolic dysfunction beyond fat mass, dental or gut infections, autoimmune activity, poor sleep, and others. A persistently high number that doesn't respond to addressing the mechanisms above is a signal to look for a different upstream cause, not a reason to add more compounds to this list. ARA-290 has a mechanistically relevant profile for this same picture (TNF-α reduction via the innate repair receptor) but isn't included here yet — its own reference card hasn't been built and verified, and this reference doesn't assert a mechanism claim it hasn't checked directly.
