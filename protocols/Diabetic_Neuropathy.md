# Diabetic Neuropathy, Retinopathy & Epithelial Damage

## The system

Diabetic peripheral neuropathy isn't caused by one thing going wrong — it's several problems compounding at once. Chronic high glucose drives the formation of advanced glycation end-products that physically damage nerve structure. The same hyperglycemic environment damages the small blood vessels feeding the nerve (the vasa nervorum), starving it of oxygen. Peripheral axons are unusually long and metabolically expensive to maintain — the mitochondria inside them and inside the Schwann cells that build the myelin sheath are working harder than almost anywhere else in the body, which makes them unusually vulnerable when that energy supply is compromised. And a large systematic review of the actual evidence found something worth taking seriously: intensive glycemic control only provides *partial* protection against neuropathy progression — lipid metabolism disturbances, particularly hypertriglyceridemia, and mitochondrial dysfunction contribute independently of blood sugar itself. This is a multi-system problem, and this reference reflects that with four separate categories of mechanism rather than one repeated compound.

## Anchor evidence — ARA-290

Worth stating plainly, because it's unusual in this reference: ARA-290 is one of the very few compounds anywhere in this app with a **completed human clinical trial in exactly this condition**, not animal work or pathway inference. A Phase 2 randomized, placebo-controlled trial gave type 2 diabetics with painful neuropathy either ARA-290 (4 mg subcutaneously daily) or placebo for 28 days, then followed them for another month off treatment. The ARA-290 group showed improved HbA1c and lipid profiles across the full 56-day observation period, significantly improved neuropathic symptoms on the PainDetect questionnaire, and a significant increase in corneal nerve fiber density — a direct structural measurement of nerve regeneration — specifically in the subjects who started with reduced density. No safety issues were identified.

This is Tier 1 evidence by this reference's own standard: peer-reviewed, human, the actual condition being asked about, not an inference from something else. One honest caveat: ARA-290 doesn't have its own full compound reference card built in this app yet — this specific evidence was verified directly against PubMed for this protocol rather than pulled from an existing card, the same practice used for Retatrutide's inflammatory-marker evidence in the Systemic Inflammation protocol. Building ARA-290's own card is a natural next step given what this trial actually shows.

## Category one — restoring blood supply to the nerve

Vasa nervorum ischemia — the small vessels that feed peripheral nerves losing function — is one of the direct mechanical drivers of nerve damage in diabetes. These two compounds approach vascular restoration from different angles.

**BPC-157**'s core mechanism (VEGFR2 internalization, eNOS release, and organized nitric-oxide-driven angiogenesis) is a direct signal for new, organized blood vessel growth — not the disorganized kind associated with tumor angiogenesis. Directly relevant here: in spinal cord compression models, BPC-157 measurably reduced the injury cascade and nerve cuts healed faster, thicker, and with less resulting pain. That's animal evidence, not a diabetic-neuropathy-specific human trial, but it's compound-specific nerve-repair data, not an inference borrowed from an unrelated tissue.

**TB-500** does the complementary job: rather than triggering the angiogenic signal itself, its actin-cytoskeleton mechanism gives endothelial and progenitor cells the physical mobility to migrate to the site, organize, and build the new vascular network the signal calls for. Angiogenesis needs both the "grow here" signal and cells that can actually move and build — this pairing covers both halves.

**GHK-Cu** contributes a third piece: copper delivered to superoxide dismutase provides direct antioxidant defense against the oxidative damage that both drives and results from microvascular injury, alongside its role supporting collagen and tissue-matrix quality in the vessel walls and surrounding tissue themselves.

## Category two — supporting the mitochondria that keep the nerve running

This is a newer addition to this protocol, grounded in the systematic-review finding above: mitochondrial dysfunction contributes to neuropathy independently of blood sugar control. Peripheral axons and the Schwann cells maintaining them are unusually energy-hungry, which makes this its own point of intervention.

**MOTS-c** drives mitochondrial biogenesis through the AMPK/PGC-1α pathway — building additional energy-production capacity in cells under chronic metabolic strain — while its insulin-independent glucose uptake mechanism also contributes to the glycemic side of the picture.

**SS-31** does a different job: rather than adding new mitochondrial capacity, it stabilizes the membrane architecture (cardiolipin) that existing mitochondria — and any new ones MOTS-c helps build — depend on to convert fuel into usable energy efficiently. Worth being precise about what this does and doesn't mean: SS-31 does not repair myelin directly. Myelin is a separate structure built by Schwann cells; SS-31's target is the mitochondria inside those cells and inside the axon itself. The connection to neuropathy is that those cells' ability to do their maintenance job depends on adequate mitochondrial function, and mitochondrial dysfunction in Schwann cells specifically has been documented as a contributor to nerve damage in diabetic models. Per this reference's own sourced protocol logic (a Trevor Bachmeyer protocol video treated as this project's highest tier for sequencing decisions), MOTS-c is run before or alongside SS-31 here — building and resetting the metabolic environment first, then stabilizing the structure once there's something worth stabilizing for.

## Category three — reducing the upstream metabolic drivers

**Retatrutide**'s contribution is upstream of the nerve itself. Its glycemic-improvement mechanism (β-cell insulin sensitization, GIP-driven insulin sensitivity) addresses the hyperglycemic driver directly — though, per the evidence above, glycemic control alone is only partial protection. More specifically relevant given that same finding: meaningful fat loss of the kind retatrutide drives is well-established to improve triglyceride levels, and hypertriglyceridemia specifically was flagged as an independent neuropathy risk factor, with triglycerides above 204 mg/dL associated with roughly 40% higher risk in the reviewed population data. No direct retatrutide-neuropathy trial exists — this is a real, traceable mechanism, not a completed outcome study, and belongs at a lower confidence tier than ARA-290's direct trial.

## Evidence tier

Worth being explicit that these seven compounds are not on equal footing, rather than implying uniform confidence: **ARA-290 stands alone with a completed human trial in this exact condition — Tier 1.** BPC-157's angiogenic mechanism is compound-specific animal nerve-repair data — Tier 2, stronger than a pathway inference but not a human neuropathy trial. TB-500's and GHK-Cu's contributions are real, sourced mechanism but one step further from the specific condition — Tier 2. MOTS-c's and SS-31's mitochondrial contributions are pathway-level evidence, sourced but not neuropathy-specific — Tier 2 leaning Tier 3. Retatrutide's contribution is the most indirect of the seven — real mechanism, no direct trial, addressing an upstream driver rather than the nerve itself — Tier 3.

## What this protocol does not cover

This pass focuses specifically on the neuropathy mechanism. Retinopathy and epithelial damage — named in this protocol's original title — draw on overlapping but not identical vascular and repair mechanisms, and deserve their own closer look rather than being assumed covered by the same compound list. Pain management is also a separate question from the repair mechanisms addressed here, though ARA-290's own trial did show a real reduction in reported neuropathic pain alongside the structural nerve finding.
