# Menopause

## The system

Menopause is a normal drop in ovarian estrogen and progesterone that most women will meet. Hot flashes, night sweats, sleep disruption, bone turnover, and a shift in how the body handles fuel are common around that change. This page is support *around* that transition. It is not a claim that menopause is undone, and it is not a substitute for a hormone conversation with a clinician.

Primary jobs and support jobs are separated on purpose. Support is still a job — just not the same job.

## What actually sits at the center

Hormone decisions — whether someone uses estrogen, progesterone, neither, or something else — belong with a licensed clinician. That is the intervention with the most direct relationship to vasomotor symptoms and bone. This app does not dose or prescribe those.

## Category one — midlife metabolic shift (primary peptide job)

Estrogen decline changes how muscle and fat handle glucose and lipids. **MOTS-c** maps here for the same AMPK / fuel-flexibility job it has on the insulin-resistance page, not because it has a hot-flash trial. If midlife weight and energy stiffness are part of the picture, this is the peptide with the cleanest reason to be first.

**NAD+** is support for that same energy chemistry (redox / sirtuins). Biochemistry is real; menopause-specific outcome data is not. Cofactor, not a second MOTS-c.

## Category two — repair backdrop (support)

**BPC-157** is not a menopause peptide. The job it *can* do here is quieter: tissues and barriers get noisier when estrogen drops (joints, gut, general inflammatory tone). BPC-157 is a repair coordinator already in this app. On this page it is a cofactor sitting under the metabolic shift, not a stand-in for estrogen.

## Category three — the less obvious hypothalamic job (support, with a catch)

**Kisspeptin** lives in KNDy neurons (kisspeptin + neurokinin B + dynorphin). Those neurons do two things that matter after estrogen falls:

1. They drive GnRH pulses (the reproductive axis).
2. They talk to the brain’s heat-dissipation wiring. KNDy activity rises after menopause, and that cluster is part of the current hot-flash biology story. The licensed hot-flash drug in this neighborhood (fezolinetant) blocks *neurokinin B*, not kisspeptin.

So kisspeptin has a real, non-obvious relationship to this system — and giving extra kisspeptin is **not** the same move as blocking NKB. Do not read this category as “kisspeptin for hot flashes.” Read it as: this pathway is involved; the peptide has no card in the app yet; if it is ever added, the job to describe is KNDy / thermoregulatory adjacency, not restarting the ovary.

## Markers people track

- Estradiol, FSH, and progesterone as a clinician frames them
- Vitamin D
- Fasting insulin / glucose if the midlife metabolic shift is the live question
- Bone-density decisions stay with a clinician

## Oral supports that actually map

- Magnesium glycinate — sleep and muscle
- Vitamin D3 and vitamin K2 — bone conversation
- Taurine / potassium — electrolyte comfort, not a hormone substitute
- Boron and P5P — thinner, optional

## Evidence tier

No human trial of MOTS-c, NAD+, or BPC-157 for hot flashes or bone in menopause. Kisspeptin’s menopause relationship is pathway-real (KNDy) and still the wrong tool if the goal is “stop the flash” the way an NK3 blocker does. This remains a lower-confidence map than insulin resistance or IBS.

## What this does not cover

Undiagnosed bleeding after menopause, HRT eligibility or contraindications, osteoporosis care, or using this list instead of evaluating thyroid, sleep apnea, or depression when those are the actual drivers.

## Related compounds

MOTS-c, NAD+, BPC-157. Kisspeptin is named as a pathway, card not built. Doses live on the cards that exist. Hormone therapy is outside this reference.
