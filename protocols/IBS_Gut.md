# IBS & Gut

## The system

The gut is four jobs that get lumped under one complaint: barrier (the lining), immune tone in that lining, motility, and the microbes living there. “IBS” is what we call it when those hurt and the workup has not found IBD, celiac, or cancer. Visceral hypersensitivity — the nerves in the gut wall yelling louder than the damage explains — is part of the picture for a lot of people.

Primary jobs and support jobs are separated on purpose.

## Category one — the lining (primary)

**BPC-157** is the center of this map. Its research conversation is actually about gut mucosa, angiogenesis in injured lining, and a local repair coordinator. Oral vs injectable is a real distinction here — oral is about reaching the gut wall directly — and that lives on the BPC card, not as a dose on this page.

Preclinical gut-injury evidence is thicker here than for the other peptides on this page. Still not a completed human IBS trial.

## Category two — quieting the mucosa (primary when the lining is angry)

**KPV** interrupts NF-κB-type inflammatory signaling. That job matches a lining that is inflamed more than a lining that is only sensitive. Post-infectious or “never settled” stories have a reason to sit KPV next to BPC. Constipation-only IBS with a calm lining does not.

## Category three — access crew and fuel (support)

**TB-500** (thymosin β4 fragment) is not an IBS drug. The job it *can* do is the same access job it has everywhere else: cell motility, getting repair cells to a damaged site, light angiogenic support. If BPC-157 is the foreman on the lining, TB-500 is the crew that shows up. Complementary on paper. Not tested as a pair in IBS.

**MOTS-c** is not a gut peptide. The job it *can* do here is the one already used in this app: repair is expensive, and a lining that cannot make ATP well repairs slowly. Fuel for the repair, not a second BPC. Borrowed from the insulin-resistance page on purpose.

## Named in the source notes — not on the public list yet

**LL-37** is a cathelicidin antimicrobial peptide. In the gut it can help or aggravate depending on context (barrier vs inflammatory flare). No card in the app. Too two-sided to put on a public IBS page until that card exists.

**Thymosin Alpha-1** is immune-modulating (T-cell). IBS is not mainly thymic aging. Possible in a post-infectious or immune-skewed story; not a default cofactor.

**Nicotine** (patch, in the source notes) hits nicotinic acetylcholine receptors, including the α7 receptor on immune cells — the cholinergic anti-inflammatory pathway. That job has human trials in **ulcerative colitis** (transdermal nicotine helped active UC in 1990s RCTs; it did not hold remission alone). Crohn’s often moves the other way with smoking. Straight IBS is thinner: population data ties heavy smoking more to IBS-diarrhea than to relief, and nicotine also speeds motility. A small 2026 open-label varenicline (nicotinic partial agonist) signal in non-constipation IBS is interesting and not a trial. So: real anti-inflammatory job on a colitis-type lining; not a default IBS tool. Named as an odd-pathway note, not a recommendation, and not dosed.

## Markers people track

- Calprotectin or CRP when the question is “is this inflammation / IBD instead?”
- Blood in stool, iron deficiency, night symptoms, or weight loss — those exit this page
- Breath testing only if a clinician is actually running a SIBO workup

## Oral supports that actually map

- Butyrate — fuel for colonocytes
- *Saccharomyces boulardii* — some human use in diarrhea-predominant pictures
- An *Akkermansia* product — emerging; not proven IBS care
- The source papers named an unspecified “SIBO compound.” It is not invented here.

## Evidence tier

**BPC-157** — Tier 2 for lining (models + mechanism).  
**KPV** — Tier 2 for mucosal inflammatory signaling.  
**TB-500 / MOTS-c** — support jobs imported from their real biology; no IBS outcome data.  
No combined human trial of this pairing.

## What this does not cover

Bloody or black stool, fever, anemia, dropping weight, new symptoms after 50, or a family history that belongs in an IBD / cancer workup. Also not a motility protocol, bile-acid diarrhea protocol, or a substitute for a diet trial a clinician already recommended.

## Related compounds

BPC-157, KPV, TB-500, MOTS-c. Doses, oral vs injectable, and cautions live on those cards.
