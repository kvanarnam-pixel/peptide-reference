What are peptides — and why use them?

Peptides are short chains of amino acids that act like biological messages. They can signal cells to change what they are doing — from repairing tissue and controlling inflammation to shifting metabolism, mitochondrial function, hormone signaling, recovery, and sleep.

Use the cards below for the fast answer. Open one when you want the deeper mechanism and the reasoning behind dose, timing, cycling, and combinations.

Signal

A peptide is often less like adding a raw ingredient and more like delivering an instruction: turn this pathway up, calm that pathway down, recruit repair, or change how a cell responds.

System

The useful question is not only “what does this peptide do?” but “what system is it changing, what happens downstream, and how does that affect the rest of the body?”

Purpose

The goal is not to chase the biggest dose or stack the most compounds. It is to use the least exposure that produces the biological result you are actually trying to achieve.

Dosing principle Minimum effective dose beats maximum tolerated dose.
Escalation principle Results earn the next step up — not the calendar.
