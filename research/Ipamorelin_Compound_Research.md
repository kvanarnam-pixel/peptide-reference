## What Happens After You Take It

### Starts

Growth hormone is your overnight repair messenger. A gland in your head — the pituitary — releases it in short waves, mostly in deep sleep.

This vial is a doorbell. One press. A short bump. Then quiet. You did not inject the messenger.

It is not an all-day faucet. If you wanted leftover signal sitting around for days, this is the wrong bottle.

### One dose

A short wave, then quiet. People say “about two hours” for how long the note hangs around — that clock comes from a vein study in people, not from the under-the-skin wellness draw. The bump itself is one episode, not a week.

### Builds

It does not leave a faucet on between nights. Extra daytime shots are more short rings, not a better single night and not a rising all-week level.

### Notice

Not a buzz. A lot of nights will feel like nothing in the moment.

Watch sleep, and how hard the next day feels. Redness at the shot is common and is not “it’s working.”

If you already draw labs, the usual number is a downstream read of the repair messenger. A rise means a note landed. It is not a trophy — and it is the wrong scoreboard if you copied a weekly faucet protocol onto this doorbell.

### Judge it

Pick two or three real things before you start — sleep, next-day effort, maybe clothes later. Judge the stretch, not the morning after the first shot. If another night note is in the same draw, you are judging the pair, not this bottle alone.

## Dose / Route / Timing / Duration

### Dose

Start low under the skin. People pass around common amounts — habit, not a study that picked the everyday dose for you.

More is not automatically better. If nothing changes after a fair stretch, do not just increase the dosage.

### Route / frequency

Under the skin. Night is the usual picture. Extra daytime shots are more waves, not a better night.

### Timing

Night. Not on a full stomach. Finish eating a couple of hours before you sleep. Wiped out or stuffed — the doorbell can click and nothing rings through.

### Duration

Give it weeks, then pause and see what held. Twelve-on / four-off — or five-on / two-off — is habit. The pause is so you can read it.

## What They're Not Telling You

**You’re buying a doorbell, not a faucet.** One press. A short bump. Then quiet. If you wanted the faucet left on all day, this isn’t that tool.

**It’s a recovery / pulse tool — not a fat burner.** Same short bump can also nudge hunger and how you store fat. Let food do the fat work.

**“Cleaner” than the older, messier cousins isn’t free.** Quieter side effects can mean you also lose whatever those messier ones were doing — including hunger some people used on purpose.

**Somebody home.** Still flat alone after sleep and food are honest? Some people add a partner on the other door. Optional, not required. No outcome trial of the exact pair.

**In animals, the more interesting story isn’t a six-pack.** Under real physical stress — illness, injury, hard training — it looks like this helps the body hold onto muscle and bone instead of burning through them. Still animal data. Not a proven human treatment. Think “stress protection,” not “get-lean peptide.”

**They tried it to wake the stomach after surgery. It didn’t win.** Still watch if your stomach acts weird. Don’t take a failed gut trial as proof it “fixes gut health.”

### TIER 2 — Technical Deep Dive

## Identity and evidence boundary

Ipamorelin is a pentapeptide GHS-R1a (ghrelin receptor) agonist (Aib-His-D-2-Nal-D-Phe-Lys-NH₂). Other door than GHRH analogs (CJC / tesamorelin). Human IV PK/PD is characterized; under-the-skin wellness PK is not published. Strongest human efficacy RCT for this molecule is a negative postoperative-ileus trial. Body-composition and fat-loss claims for wellness use are not supported by an ipamorelin-specific adult RCT.

## Key citations (load-bearing)

- Raun et al. 1998 — swine endocrine selectivity vs other GHRPs. PMID 9861692.
- Gobburu et al. 1999 — human IV PK/PD; t½ ≈ 2 h; single GH episode. PMID 10389872.
- Beck et al. 2014 — Phase 2 postoperative ileus RCT; primary endpoint not met. PMID 25192402.
- Lall et al. 2001 — mouse adiposity / GH-independent signals caution against fat-burner framing.
- Andersen et al. 2001 — adult rat glucocorticoid model; bone formation / muscle strength context. PMID 11735244.
- Svensson / Johansen bone-mineral line (e.g. PMID 10828840) — animal BMC context; not human rebuild proof.
- Rosenthal et al. 1986 — exogenous GH blunts GRF-induced GH. PMID 3080474.
- Hartman et al. 1993 — IGF-I feedback / somatostatin involvement in GH axis.
- NCT01280344 — dose-finding registry; results unpublished.
- Hexarelin / CD36 cardiac stories do not transfer to this vial — competitive positioning only; do not put on the spoken-face card.
