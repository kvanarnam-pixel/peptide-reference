## What Happens After You Take It

You swallow it. That morning is blank. No buzz. No crash. No “oh, there it is” the way a stimulant or a weekly weight shot can give you one.

Inside fat cells there is a little toll booth. It skims raw material away from the energy-carrier supply before the cell can use it. This pill blocks the booth. More of that raw material stays in play.

You will not feel that. The earliest honest look is a week or two, and even that is usually “same food, same workouts, pants feeling a little different.” The real look, if there is one, is more like a month — often six to eight weeks.

If you need to feel something this week to stay with it, this is the wrong one.

## It is a pill, not a peptide

Almost everything else in this app is a shot. This one is built small enough to swallow. That is the whole practical reason it exists.

Swallowable is not the same as “we proved the breakfast capsule in people.” We did not. In mice, almost none of a swallowed dose showed up in the blood. In rats, more did. Nobody has measured a person. The studies that made this famous were mostly shots under the skin.

The question worth asking at the table is not “is a pill safer.” It is: the booth this blocks also spends a tag the cell uses to flip genes on and off. Do you scramble other tags when you shut the booth?

Two plain answers:

The first paper on this chemical family checked nearby tagging tools and the normal battery-recycling tools. It did not knock those over at the amounts that matter. That is reassuring. It is not a human safety study.

In fat, shutting the booth is the point — the cell keeps more of its raw material and burns a little more oxygen. In one kind of mouth cancer, the same booth running *hot* in the scar-tissue around the tumor helped the tumor. Turning that booth down slowed it. Opposite of the usual “could this help something we don’t want.” One cancer type. Not a reason to take this for cancer. Not a reason to panic about gene tags either.

## Executive identity

**5-Amino-1MQ is the NAD+ supply-chain guard.** Not a peptide. Not a new instruction. It blocks nicotinamide N-methyltransferase (NNMT), which would otherwise divert a NAD+ precursor into 1-methylnicotinamide before the cell can use it. It also inhibits MAO-A off-target — a second job that does not fit the “guard” picture and should not be forced into it.

Construction model:

- **5-Amino-1MQ:** supply-chain guard. Second, less-publicized job (MAO-A) sits beside that, not inside it.
- **NAD+:** rechargeable power carrier.
- **MOTS-c:** power-plant program (muscle).
- **SS-31:** wiring clamp.
- **Retatrutide:** load and routing.
- **Tesamorelin:** GH-axis visceral-fat signal.

## Identity and evidence boundary

Every piece of direct evidence on this exact compound is in rodents. No published human trial.

1. **Target validation (genetic, mouse):** Kraus et al. (2014, *Nature*) knocked down NNMT in adipose and liver. NNMT was the most reciprocally regulated gene in adipose comparing Glut4-knockout to Glut4-overexpressing mice. Expression rises in white fat and liver of obese and diabetic mice. The introduction cites earlier work — elevated NNMT in adipocytes of obese vs non-obese Pima Indians, and elevated urinary 1-methylnicotinamide in type 2 diabetes — background from other groups, not a new human finding from this paper. Brown-fat weight, core temperature, and UCP1 protein in brown fat were measured and unchanged.
2. **Small-molecule class:** Neelakantan et al. (2018). Membrane-permeable methylquinolinium NNMT inhibitors, including 5-amino-1MQ. Selective against related methyltransferases and NAD-salvage enzymes. Adipocytes: less 1-MNA, more NAD+ and SAM, less lipogenesis. DIO mice: 20 mg/kg SC three times daily for 11 days; less body weight and white-fat mass; food intake unchanged.
3. **This molecule, 28 days + PK:** Babula et al. (2024). 5A1MQ daily 28 days in DIO mice: less weight and fat-mass gain, better oral glucose tolerance, less hyperinsulinemia, less hepatic steatosis, ALT/AST/ketones closer to normal. PK: IV terminal half-life 6.3 h; SC 25 mg/kg terminal half-life ~13 h, Cmax 7,010 ng/mL; oral 30 mg/kg terminal half-life ~14.8 h, Cmax 14.5 ng/mL at 4 h, oral bioavailability **3.5%**. High exposure in adipose, muscle, liver after SC. Cerep panel at 10 μM: no significant hit except **67.4% MAO-A inhibition**. Authors note that could partly explain the glucose-tolerance change; they did not settle how much. They also flag MAO-A as an off-target they want to design out. Oral poor absorption attributed to limited enteric uptake plus first-pass (hepatocyte half-life <7 min).
4. **Rat PK:** Awosemo et al. (2021). 5-AMQ in Sprague-Dawley rats: IV half-life 3.8 h, oral 6.9 h, oral bioavailability **38.4%**. No human PK to say which species is closer.
5. **Practice:** 50–100 mg oral daily; 8-on/4-off; “1–2 weeks to notice, ~2 months to judge.” None of the studies used a human, that oral milligram, or that calendar.

Live-card `halfLife: "4–6 hours"` is unsourced. Closest published figure is rat oral 6.9 h. Not changed this pass.

## Core cascade

**5-Amino-1MQ → NNMT inhibition in adipocytes → nicotinamide and SAM not consumed into 1-MNA → higher NAD+ and SAM → higher polyamine flux via ODC / SSAT / PAO → higher oxygen consumption → less lipogenesis, less fat-mass gain, better glucose handling in the mouse.**  
**Separately:** MAO-A inhibition off-target. Mechanism and in-person consequence not characterized beyond the panel screen.

### NNMT as a diversion enzyme

NNMT methylates nicotinamide with SAM and produces 1-MNA, which is excreted. One nicotinamide leaves NAD salvage; one SAM is spent on a reaction that does not feed energy metabolism. Some papers also treat 1-MNA as a signal, not only an exit. This card claims the salvage leak. It does not claim 1-MNA is meaningless.

### Genetic validation

Kraus: *Nnmt* knockdown in WAT and liver protected mice from diet-induced obesity by raising energy expenditure — about 47% less relative fat mass, 15% more relative lean mass, ~60% higher adipocyte oxygen consumption. Higher adipose SAM and NAD+. Higher ODC and SSAT via H3K4 methylation effects. Higher urinary and adipocyte diacetylspermine. BAT weight, core temperature, and UCP1 protein in BAT unchanged. ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature13198))

Polyamine-flux oxygen use. Not UCP1 browning. The negative UCP1 measurement is direct evidence against that framing.

### Small-molecule class

Neelakantan: high permeability, selective vs related methyltransferases and NAD-salvage enzymes. Adipocytes: less 1-MNA, more NAD+/SAM, less lipogenesis. DIO mice 20 mg/kg SC TID × 11 days: less weight and WAT mass, smaller adipocytes, lower plasma cholesterol, food intake unchanged. Discussion speculates thermogenic genes *could* be looked at later. Not measured there. Kraus already measured UCP1 and it did not move. ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bcp.2017.11.007))

### This molecule + PK + off-target

Babula 2024 as above. 28-day mouse. Not a human trial. Not multi-month human use. Not proof the community capsule matches the exposure that produced those readouts. ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/dom.15879))

### Rat oral absorption

Awosemo 2021: 38.4% oral bioavailability in rat vs 3.5% in mouse. ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpba.2021.114255))

### Tumor stroma — separate context

Zhao 2023: stromal NNMT up in OSCC; NNMT-high CAFs accelerated tumor growth via NNMT–LOX–FAK and collagen crosslinking. ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41388-023-02642-5))  
Wang 2024: NNMT in fibroblasts → ETS2–VEGFA angiogenesis. ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41388-024-03112-2))  
Holkom 2024: CAFs secrete more VEGFA than the cancer cells; NNMT block cut angiogenesis markers in assembled organoids. ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/odi.14945))  
Do not merge with the fat-cell cascade. Do not stretch into an oncology claim.
