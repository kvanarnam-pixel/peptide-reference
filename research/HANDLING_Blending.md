# Handling — Blending vs Stacking

## Blending vs stacking

**Stacking** means separate vials, each on its own dose and schedule.  
**Blending** means several peptides in one vial — Wolverine (BPC-157 + TB-500), GLOW (that pair + GHK-Cu), KLOW (GLOW + KPV), or a GH-axis dual like CJC-1295 no-DAC + Ipamorelin.

We treat pre-mixed vials as **convenience products**, not better science.

### Why repair blends fail the math
- **Different clocks** — BPC / GHK-style use is often daily; TB-500 is often a few times a week. One shot cannot obey both.
- **Different dose units** — BPC is usually hundreds of mcg; TB-500 and GHK-Cu are milligrams. A 5/5 or 50/10/10 vial is factory weight, not a protocol.
- **You lose the experiment** — one volume moves three jobs; you cannot tell which signal earned its place.
- **GHK-Cu is extra** — copper in a shared stored vial is an avoidable chemistry risk. Keep GHK-Cu in its own vial.

### Named blends
| Name | Contents | House read |
|------|----------|------------|
| Wolverine | BPC-157 + TB-500 | Schedule + unit clash |
| GLOW | Wolverine + GHK-Cu | Same + copper in the pot |
| KLOW | GLOW + KPV | Four knobs glued together |
| CJC no-DAC + Ipamorelin | GH-axis dual | Least incoherent on dose/timing; still convenience. Separate vials preferred |

Pharmacy multi-peptide vials follow the **same** rule as research blends. “The compounder mixed it” does not fix clocks, ratios, or copper.

### What we do instead
Separate vials. Separate reconstitution.  

**Same-syringe (optional convenience):** only when more than one compound is actually due **that day**, drawn so vials stay pure, injected **immediately**.  
**Do not** use one needle for two vials — residue on the needle contaminates the second vial for the rest of its life. Prefer: draw A → change needle → draw B → inject, or two separate shots. Never return mixed liquid to either vial.  

**Do not** pour reconstituted vials together for storage.  
**Do not** co-draw GLP-1 / dual / triple agonists (semaglutide, tirzepatide, retatrutide, etc.) with these research peptides.  
**Already on a blend?** Prefer switch to separate vials at the next bottle. Do not “fix harder” on a fixed ratio.

### What happens if they are blended anyway

**In the vial**  
Peptides share one liquid. They can sit quietly for a while if cold and used soon — or nudge pH, haze, or (with GHK-Cu) speed oxidation of partners, or lose copper to a thiol (glutathione, NAC). Vendor “still pure at 30 days” is not proof each peptide still does its job like a single vial.

**Under the skin**  
They share a depot, then absorb as separate small peptides. You did not make a new drug. More volume and copper can mean more site irritation. You cannot tell which peptide caused redness.

**In the body**  
Each still has its own job. Complementary mechanisms are a stacking story — not a measured extra effect from sharing a vial. There are no controlled human trials of Wolverine, GLOW, or KLOW as combinations. Outcomes people report are those peptides at locked doses, plus sleep, food, and rehab — not a proven “blend multiplier.”

### — Technical Deep Dive divider —

## Evidence boundary
No controlled human combination trials for Wolverine, GLOW, or KLOW. Synergy is mechanistic inference from single-agent preclinical work, not combo superiority data. Vendor HPLC of reconstituted blends is quality-control anecdote, not peer-reviewed head-to-head vs singles stored alone.

## Chemical processes in co-solution
- pH / buffer competition between sequences  
- Hydrolysis / deamidation outside preferred windows  
- Aggregation at air–water interface (shaking, vortex)  
- **Metal-catalyzed oxidation (GHK-Cu):** Cu(II) can participate in redox cycling; residues at risk include Cys, Met, His, Trp, Tyr. BPC-157 lacks Cys/Met; TB-500/Tβ4 contains Met. Tight GHK–Cu binding ≠ zero catalytic risk over stored time  
- **Thiol displacement:** GSH/NAC can form ternary Cu complexes and strip copper from GHK; hard incompatibility  
- Chloride/saline can compete for Cu(II) — poor recon choice for copper peptides  
- **Time scale:** minutes in a syringe ≠ days–weeks in a fridge vial

## Cross-contamination on draw
Puncturing vial B with a needle that has contacted vial A introduces residual peptide A into B. Alcohol on the stopper does not remove lumen/hub residue. Cumulative adulteration of the second vial is independent of “chemical compatibility.” Mitigation: new needle per vial, or separate injections.

## Pharmacokinetics of co-injection
Small peptides absorb from SC tissue via capillaries ± lymph; co-location in one depot does not create documented systemic PK synergy. Larger volume increases local discomfort risk. No published human PK of GLOW/KLOW/Wolverine vs separate vials.

## Pharmacodynamics
Complementary construction roles (foreman, access crew, materials/blueprints, site safety) support **stacking** on independent schedules. Combination superiority at locked blend ratios is untested in humans.

## Scope
This document covers **injectable** co-formulation and co-draw. Topical or oral combinations are out of scope here.
