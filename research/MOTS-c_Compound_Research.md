# MOTS-c Compound Research

## Executive identity

**MOTS-c is the power plant.** A 16-amino-acid mitochondrial-derived peptide, encoded in mtDNA, that tells the cell to take up glucose without waiting on insulin and to build more mitochondria. It does not coordinate the repair job, move crews, or deliver structural materials — it supplies the energy capacity those jobs consume.

Construction model:

- **TB-500:** scaffolding + access crew - reorganizes cellular scaffolding, helps crews move, opens routes into damaged tissue.
- **BPC-157:** foreman - coordinates the local repair environment and rebuild.
- **GHK-Cu:** materials and blueprints - delivers copper, resets gene-expression instructions.
- **MOTS-c:** power plant - supports the cellular energy capacity needed to execute repair.

## Identity and evidence boundary

MOTS-c's evidence problem is **mouse-to-human dose convention plus a cycling story that got the mechanism wrong.** Read in layers:

1. **Foundational mouse metabolic evidence:** Lee et al. 2015 (Cell Metabolism) — MOTS-c promotes metabolic homeostasis and reduces obesity and insulin resistance in mice. This is the paper for those claims. Do not park them on later papers.
2. **Exercise / aging physical-performance evidence:** Reynolds et al. 2021 (Nature Communications) — exercise-induced mitochondrial-encoded regulator of age-dependent physical decline in mice, with intermittent 3×/week dosing. This paper's finding is exercise/aging physical performance. Do not attribute obesity findings to it.
3. **Pathway papers (AMPK, PGC-1α, NF-κB):** Diabetologia 2020 (PGC-1α/AMPK-dependent mitochondrial bioenergetic improvement — this is 2020 Vol 63 pp 2675–2688, not a 2016 paper); Shen et al. 2021 and Yan et al. 2019 for NF-κB suppression in cardiac cells and a mouse model.
4. **Human injectable evidence:** essentially absent. 5 mg SC 2–3×/week is practitioner convention, not a controlled human dose-finding result.
5. **Observed household case:** improvement at ~6.5 weeks on, full reversal ~2 weeks after stopping, requiring restart. Physiologically consistent with detraining. Not a formal study.

Mechanism earns inclusion; evidence type determines wording. Several previously circulating citations (including "Oncogene 2020" and "Diabetologia 2016") were fabricated or mismatched and are not used here.

## Core biological cascade

**MOTS-c → folate-cycle inhibition → AICAR accumulation → AMPK activation → GLUT4 translocation (insulin-independent glucose uptake) + PGC-1α-driven mitochondrial biogenesis + NF-κB/NLRP3 suppression.**

### Folate cycle, AICAR, and AMPK
MOTS-c inhibits the folate cycle. AICAR builds up. AMPK turns on. That is an enzymatic route, not a receptor-ligand event. There is no MOTS-c receptor in this cascade to desensitize, which is why "cycle it so the receptor resets" is a mechanism mismatch and must not appear on the card.

### GLUT4 and insulin-independent glucose uptake
AMPK activation moves GLUT4 to the muscle-cell surface. Glucose walks in without needing insulin. This is the clearest metabolic job of the molecule and the reason "exercise mimetic" is not marketing language — it is a description of the pathway.

### PGC-1α and mitochondrial biogenesis
AMPK/PGC-1α signaling drives mitochondrial bioenergetic improvement (Diabetologia 2020). MOTS-c's distinctive long-arc job is building new power plants, not just flipping AMPK for a few hours. That is also why pairing it with a membrane-protection peptide is a protocol question rather than a stacking flourish.

### NF-κB / NLRP3
NF-κB suppression is reported in cardiac cells (Shen 2021) and a mouse model (Yan 2019). Treat as pathway evidence, not a human anti-inflammatory indication.

### What this cascade does not include
**Hepatic FGF-21 upregulation as a benefit is unsupported and likely backwards.** Heldens et al. 2025 found FGF-21 and MOTS-c both correlate *with* liver-disease severity. That is not MOTS-c causing FGF-21 upregulation as a therapeutic effect. Do not smuggle it back into mechanism or benefits language.

### Cancer — scoped, not generalized
Yin et al. 2024 (Advanced Science) is ovarian-cancer-specific. General AMPK/mTOR tumor-suppression biology is a separate, broader frame. Do not cite "Oncogene 2020" — it does not exist. Do not convert one ovarian-cancer paper into a MOTS-c oncology claim.

## One genuine non-obvious insight

MOTS-c is an exercise mimetic in the literal sense: **gains behave like training adaptations — maintenance-dependent, not permanent.** Stop the signal and the adaptation detains. A household case showed real improvement at 6.5 weeks on and full reversal about two weeks after stopping, requiring restart. That is detraining physiology, not proof the cycle "failed." Consumer cycling language that talks about receptor resets is describing a different class of molecule.

## Practical dosing/cycling logic

**Popular claim:** 5–10 mg SC 2–3×/week; 8–12 weeks on / 4 months off; max 3 cycles per year.
**Real source:** none of the human milligram numbers or the long off-period are trial-derived. Reynolds 2021 used intermittent 3×/week dosing in mice for exercise/aging performance, not a human milligram protocol. Lee 2015 is mouse metabolic work.
**More defensible statement:** 5 mg SC, 2–3×/week is the working practitioner convention. Short plasma exposure, longer downstream AMPK/PGC-1α signaling — more frequent is not automatically more biology.

### Cycling — corrected

**8–12 weeks on / 1–2 weeks off, run WITH SS-31 throughout (not optional — see Synergy).**

The rationale is **not** receptor desensitization. MOTS-c's mechanism is enzymatic/AICAR-driven AMPK activation, not receptor-ligand binding, so there is no receptor to desensitize. That framing is a mechanism mismatch.

Actual reasoning: MOTS-c is an exercise mimetic. Gains behave like training adaptations — maintenance-dependent, not permanent. Observed case: household use showed real improvement at 6.5 weeks on, but full reversal ~2 weeks after stopping, requiring restart. This matches known detraining physiology, not treatment failure.

The off-period is a **monitoring/reassessment checkpoint** (labs, subjective response, injection-site/allergic-reaction check) — not a required biological reset.

Classification: **Evidence-limited/conservative + Protocol convention + Observed case (physiologically consistent, not a formal study).**

### Trial-derived vs practical

- **Mouse / paper pattern:** intermittent 3×/week (Reynolds 2021 — exercise/aging physical performance in mice). Lee 2015 is the obesity/insulin-resistance mouse paper.
- **Practical (what's on the card):** 5 mg SC, 2–3×/week.
- **Why they differ:** 5 mg is empirical human convention, not a scaled-and-validated human dose-finding number. Use the practical range for real protocols; do not quietly "correct" it toward a mouse milligram-per-kilogram figure, and do not hang obesity claims on Reynolds.

## What to expect and monitor

### Early (days–2 weeks)
Possible shift in energy or glucose handling as AMPK/GLUT4 engages. Not a stimulant-type kick.

### Building (through ~6–8 weeks)
Mitochondrial biogenesis is slower than the AMPK flip. Observed household improvement showed up around 6.5 weeks on. Function and training tolerance are better scorecards than whether an injection "feels like something."

### Longer term (through 8–12 weeks, then the short off)
Adaptations are maintenance-dependent. Expect fade after stopping — that is detraining, not proof the run failed. Use the 1–2 week off as a checkpoint: labs, subjective response, injection-site or allergic-reaction check. Then decide whether to restart.

## Common mistakes

1. Calling the off-period a receptor reset. There is no receptor in this cascade to desensitize.
2. Treating the post-stop fade as treatment failure instead of detraining.
3. Running MOTS-c as a mitochondrial rebuild without SS-31 in a protocol that claims to keep what was built.
4. Dosing more often only because plasma half-life is short. Downstream AMPK/PGC-1α signaling outlasts circulating peptide.
5. Attributing obesity/insulin-resistance findings to Reynolds 2021. That paper is exercise/aging physical performance.
6. Repeating "hepatic FGF-21 upregulation" as a MOTS-c benefit. Heldens 2025 has FGF-21 and MOTS-c correlating with liver-disease severity — likely backwards as a benefit claim.
7. Generalizing Yin 2024 (ovarian cancer) into a broad oncology story, or citing "Oncogene 2020."

## Detraining-vs-reset nuance

The honest open question is not "does MOTS-c need an off-period so a receptor can recover." It doesn't work that way.

The real distinction: **training physiology vs. receptor pharmacology.** Stop an exercise-mimetic signal and the adaptation detains on a short clock (weeks, not months). The 1–2 week off is therefore a monitoring checkpoint, not a four-month biological exile. The older "8–12 weeks on / 4 months off, max 3 cycles per year" calendar treated this like a hormone you must wash out. That over-corrected a problem this cascade does not have, and it under-protected the actual problem (keeping what you built — hence SS-31 alongside).

Human injectable dose-finding and multi-year safety data do not exist. Treat 5 mg / 2–3× weekly / 8–12 week courses as conservative convention, not a proven-safe protocol.

## What They're Not Telling You

Most MOTS-c cycling advice quietly imports receptor-reset language from a different class of peptides. AMPK does not work like that. The fade after stopping is what you would expect from an exercise mimetic — it is not a scandal and it is not a reason to invent a four-month off. Separately, "FGF-21 upregulation" has been sold as a liver benefit; the 2025 liver-disease data has MOTS-c and FGF-21 moving *with* severity, which is not that story. And FDA has not "approved MOTS-c for compounding": staff recommended against 503A listing, PCAC voted in favor in July 2026, and no final rule has issued.
